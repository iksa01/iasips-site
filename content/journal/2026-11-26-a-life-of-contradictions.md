The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949, seventy-seven years ago today. The evening before, B. R. Ambedkar warned the Constituent Assembly that political democracy would not last unless social democracy lay under it: a way of life built on liberty, equality and fraternity, held together as one.

::: quote
On the 26th of January 1950, we are going to enter into a life of contradictions.

<cite>B. R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 November 1949</cite>
:::

The contradiction: in politics, one man one vote and one vote one value; in social and economic life, no such thing as one man one value.

## Not three items, one union

Liberty, equality and fraternity, he said, are not separate items in a trinity. Divorce one from the others and democracy loses its purpose.

> Without equality, liberty would produce the supremacy of the few over the many. Equality without liberty would kill individual initiative.

<cite>B. R. Ambedkar</cite>

Liberty alone becomes the freedom of the strong. Equality alone becomes sameness, with no reason to strive. And without fraternity, even the two together would not come naturally. They would need a constable to enforce them.

## Fraternity is the key

Fraternity is the sense that all Indians are one people. It is what gives social life its unity, and it is the hardest of the three. Liberty and equality can be written into law. Fraternity cannot. It has to be felt.

Ambedkar told a story from James Bryce: an American church once adopted the prayer “O Lord, bless our nation”, then dropped it the next day because its members objected to the word *nation*. If Americans could not yet feel they were a nation, he asked, how could a people divided into thousands of castes? Castes separate social life and breed jealousy between caste and caste. To believe we were already a nation was a delusion. Only by admitting we were not would we work to become one.

::: quote
Without fraternity equality and liberty will be no deeper than coats of paint.

<cite>B. R. Ambedkar</cite>
:::
