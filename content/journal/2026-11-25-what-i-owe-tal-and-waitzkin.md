The [World Chess Championship](https://www.fide.com/gukesh-and-sindarov-set-for-world-championship-showdown/) is on: Gukesh is defending his title against Javokhir Sindarov. It sent me back to two chess masters I owe a lot to.

::: quote
You must take your opponent into a deep dark forest where 2+2=5, and the path leading out is only wide enough for one.

<cite>Mikhail Tal</cite>
:::

I am no chess expert. I can barely tell d4 from h8. But in Tal’s games, as Mato Jelić explains them, Tal seems to see only one piece on the board: the opponent’s king. Every move is directed at it.

That is an anchor. Tal taught me that energy should be long-term, and should move in one direction. I never noticed when I picked up the habit, but over the years it became mine.

The second master is Josh Waitzkin, the author of [*The Art of Learning*](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=The+Art+of+Learning+Josh+Waitzkin). I learnt the basics from him on Chessmaster, long before I found [Lichess](https://lichess.org/). Today I found all his Chessmaster lessons in one playlist, and I am thankful to the person who put it together.

Chess can teach you a lot about life, pattern recognition above all. It is a beautiful game, and it changes you from the inside out.

Thank you, Lichess. Thank you, Mikhail Tal. Thank you, Mato Jelić. Thank you, Josh Waitzkin. And above all, thank you to my dad, the first person I saw playing chess. He is the one who set me in this direction.
